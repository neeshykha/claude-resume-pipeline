## skill-regression-harness
**URL:** _(local only as of 2026-08-28 — do not cite in an application until it is pushed public)_
**What:** A regression suite for AI agent skill definitions, which are prompt-matched rather than called — nothing imports them, nothing type-checks them, and nothing fails loudly when one stops working, so a model upgrade can silently stop a production skill from firing. Two layers: free static checks (frontmatter completeness, name/directory agreement, duplicate names, trigger phrases claimed by more than one skill, and every filesystem path a skill tells the agent to read), and live dispatch testing of 19 frozen prompts across six confusable groups, five of which expect *no* skill to fire. Safety is by construction: every dispatch runs under a tool allowlist permitting only skill loading, so skills that depend on live Salesforce and Gmail get routing-tested without touching either — verified when the Salesforce skill dispatched, attempted its query, and was blocked. Live run: 18/19 correct, 0 errored. Scoring reuses claude-eval-kit, making skill dispatch its second domain.
**Cite when:** JD asks about AI governance, LLM reliability or evaluation in production, post-deployment monitoring, agent QA, regression testing of AI systems, or "how do you keep AI working after you ship it." This is the strongest answer to the AI-governance language now common in the funnel (Alston's "governance standards for responsible, secure, compliant AI use"; GitLab AI Transformation Owner's AI evaluation and piloting). It covers the half of AI ops that deflection-audit and claude-eval-kit do not: not whether the AI was right, but whether the system still routes to it at all. The safety design (allowlist so a test can't trigger the side effects it's testing for) is the talking point for anyone asking how you test against production systems without risk. Also strong evidence of honest engineering — three defects were found in the harness itself by running it, one of which was hiding the suite's only real finding behind a spurious error.
**Resume line:** Built a regression suite for AI agent skill definitions combining static validation with live dispatch testing under a tool allowlist, catching silent routing failures that model upgrades introduce; found and fixed three defects in the harness itself, including one masking a genuine finding.

## deflection-audit
**URL:** https://github.com/neeshykha/deflection-audit
**What:** Audits the deflection rate an AI support deployment reports. Vendors count deflection as conversations the AI closed without a human handoff, which counts closures rather than resolutions — abandonment, re-contacts, and later escalations all look identical to a success. This re-judges every AI-closed conversation from the transcript alone (at close time, without seeing the follow-up signal), then recomputes the rate crediting only closures that hold up. On a 40-conversation labeled set: 80.0% claimed vs. 55.0% audited, a 25-point overstatement. Two layers by design — the auditor is scored against ground truth first (25/32 outcomes, 8/11 confusable-pattern traps) before its numbers are trusted. Ships a static HTML report, a reusable QA rubric, and 18 unit tests covering the deterministic half.
**Cite when:** JD asks about measuring AI deployment success, post-launch adoption or ROI, deflection/containment metrics, AI quality assurance, or "how do you know the AI is actually working." Especially strong for Deployment Strategist, AI Engagement/Enablement, and AI Operations roles — it answers the rollout-health question the other repos don't touch. Also the best evidence of metric skepticism applied to a number he personally owns (85% deflection via Maven AGI at iApts). Pairs with claude-eval-kit as proof that kit generalizes to a second domain.
**Resume line:** Built an audit tool measuring true vs. reported AI support deflection, finding a 25-point overstatement on a labeled conversation set by re-judging closures on whether the customer's problem was actually resolved.

## sf-caseops-mcp
**URL:** https://github.com/neeshykha/sf-caseops-mcp

Auth is delegated entirely to the `sf` CLI keychain; read-only is enforced by construction (only wraps query/describe subcommands, SELECT-only guard on raw SOQL). **Cite when:** JD asks about hands-on integration building, agent tooling, MCP, connecting LLMs to enterprise systems (CRM especially), Salesforce technical depth beyond declarative admin, or SLA/entitlement management. This is the direct answer to the "integration building" gap flagged in Solutions Engineer JDs — an agent-standard integration, not a one-off script. Also strong for AI Engagement/Deployment roles asking how AI reaches production systems safely (the read-only-by-construction design is the talking point), and for support-ops roles asking how SLA risk gets surfaced before it becomes a breach.
**Resume line:** Built an MCP server exposing live Salesforce case operations as AI-agent-callable tools, with credential handling delegated to the platform CLI and read-only access enforced by construction; extended it with an SLA milestone risk layer serving both conversational AI access and an always-on operations dashboard.

## claude-resume-pipeline
**URL:** https://github.com/neeshykha/claude-resume-pipeline
**What:** AI-powered resume tailoring and job discovery pipeline. CLAUDE.md encodes ATS optimization logic; Python handles ATS board polling (50+ companies), composite scoring, and PDF rendering. Also ships **`audit_scores.py`**, a calibration audit that re-derives the pipeline's own scores from the rubric that produced them. Because half the rubric is judgment (keyword overlap, reach penalties) and half is mechanical (title tier, source quality, company bonuses, salary band, freshness), it computes a *legal envelope* per job rather than a single right answer: a score outside its envelope is provably wrong, and a score inside it is reported with the judgment it would require, for a human to weigh. It also detects **rubric drift** — jobs scored under a retired version of the rules whose recorded tailoring tier the current rules would not grant. On 205 scored rows it found 15 still-queued roles carrying a location bonus a rule change had already retired, five of them sitting a full tier above where today's rubric puts them. The auditor is scored against hand-adjudicated cases (6/6) before its output is trusted.
**Cite when:** JD mentions AI tooling implementation, workflow automation, encoding domain expertise into AI systems, or — for the audit half — measurement discipline, scoring model governance, config drift, or QA of an automated decision system. The audit is the strongest part to cite for roles that care whether someone checks their own automation: it audits a scoring model he built and depends on, and it deliberately reports what it *cannot* prove rather than manufacturing certainty. Use carefully — citing the resume pipeline *in a resume it produced* can be a clever meta-point or too cute, judge per company culture.
**Resume line:** Designed an AI job-discovery and resume-tailoring pipeline encoding ATS screening logic into a Claude instruction layer, with automated polling of 50+ company job boards; added a calibration audit that re-derives every score from the rubric and flags decisions made under superseded scoring rules, surfacing 15 queued items whose tier no longer matched current criteria.

## piedmont-bloom-planner
**URL:** https://github.com/neeshykha/piedmont-bloom-planner — live at https://neeshykha.github.io/piedmont-bloom-planner/
**What:** A native-plant planting planner for the Georgia Piedmont that solves for continuous bloom rather than listing species. Takes bed dimensions, sun, soil moisture, soil texture, and a height cap; filters a 41-species database to what will actually grow there; and runs a greedy solver picking a set that keeps at least two species flowering every month from March through November. Computes quantities from mature spread against bed area. The design decision worth naming: it separates bloom gaps you can close (an eligible plant covers that month, just unselected) from gaps your constraints made impossible, and for the latter it names the binding constraint. Encodes real regional judgment — heavy clay is treated as a first-class constraint, and the tool warns at three severities that butterfly weed, the default monarch recommendation everywhere, crown-rots in wet Atlanta clay. Single self-contained HTML file, no dependencies, no network calls, runs from `file://`.
**Cite when:** ONLY where personal building, curiosity, or "what do you make for yourself" is genuinely relevant — startups, AI-native companies, product-minded teams, or an interview asking what he builds outside work. Its real strength is that a reviewer can click a link and use it in ten seconds with nothing installed, which no other repo in the set offers. **Do not cite it** for AI deployment, evaluation rigor, Python depth, or enterprise systems work; there is no AI component and no test suite. Never lead with it. Same narrow lane as Stride-App, but more polished and actually usable by a stranger.
**Resume line:** Built a browser-based native-plant planner that solves for unbroken seasonal bloom coverage, encoding regional soil and horticultural constraints into a dependency-free single-file tool.
